## Facts

- Elkhorn named for interlocked antlers of two elk found at the site; Eaton's advice: Hagedorn (1921), from interviews with Ferris, Merrifield and Sewall.
- Left New York July 28, reached the Maltese Cross Aug. 1, 1884; worked cattle until near the end of August, then moved downriver: Sewall. (The NPS stop text says Sewall and Dow "arrived by train" on August 25; Sewall's own account differs, so the guide follows Sewall.)
- Mittie died about 3 a.m., Alice about twelve hours later, Feb. 14, 1884: Sewall prints a letter giving these times. Alice had given birth two days earlier, Feb. 12.
- Large X drawn on the diary page: **verify against the diary on loan (Susan Sarna).**
- About a thousand more head put on the range, 1884: Hagedorn.
- Dow went East June 21, 1885 to marry and bring back Sewall's wife: Hagedorn.
- Toys for Sewall's daughter (picture blocks, letter blocks, a little horse, a rag doll), requested from "Bamie" (Anna): Hagedorn.
- Two babies a week apart, doctor 110 miles away, the cradle: Sewall.
- Fall 1886 Chicago sale at a loss; Sewall and Dow advise T.R. to get out; families leave: Sewall.

## Open items before wide promotion

1. Susan Sarna sign-off on all quotations and on the diary "X."
2. NPS photo credits: the API supplies no credit for six of the eight stop photos. They are captioned "Photo courtesy of the National Park Service" and should be confirmed with the park.
3. The route line joins the stop coordinates and only approximates the mowed path. Walk it once with the guide open and adjust if a segment misleads.
