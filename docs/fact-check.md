# Elkhorn Ranch Walking Guide: fact check

Revised October 2026, when the guide gained photo galleries, "Look for" cues, a "More at this stop" section on each stop, the stolen-boat story and a then-and-now comparison. Every quotation was compared word for word against archive.org scans of the first edition (two scans where two exist), and the whole file was then checked again by a separate reviewer who had not written it.

## Corrections made to the earlier version

1. **Stop 8, 1892 letter.** The old guide quoted "The old ranch is as picturesque as ever; but it is melancholy to see it deserted," as a letter to Anna Roosevelt, Aug. 11, 1892. That wording could not be found in Hagedorn, Mattison, Sewall or *Letters to Anna Roosevelt Cowles* (1924). Replaced with the documented 1892 letter to Sewall (SW pp. 100–101).
2. **Stop 4.** Sewall and Dow did not work cattle at the Maltese Cross for most of August 1884; Sewall says they started north with a herd the day after arriving (SW pp. 17–18).
3. **Stop 4.** "Sewall drew up the plan" became "Sewall later claimed the design." A rough plan in T.R.'s hand led NPS historians to credit T.R. with the basic design (HSR p. 57). Added that T.R.'s photographs show a low-pitched shingled roof, not Sewall's "flat roof."
4. **Stop 6.** *Hunting Trips* was finished before the house (its note is dated May 1885). The book T.R. wrote here was *Thomas Hart Benton* (1886).
5. **Stop 7.** Dow left for Maine in June 1885 but returned with the families in August (Mattison p. 54, citing Sewall's letter of Aug. 16, 1885). Lede now says "late summer."
6. **Stop 1.** The naming story is single-source (Hagedorn) and is now attributed to him in the copy.
7. **Stop 3.** "The cattle wintered well. Sewall went to New York" reversed the order; Sewall went that spring, and T.R. learned how the herd had wintered in June.

## Facts

- Arrival July 28 / Aug. 1, 1884: SW p. 14. Hewing began October 1884: SW p. 19; Mattison p. 53. Shingled by Feb. 1885: Sewall letter in Mattison p. 53.
- Logs hewn on three sides, some over 30 ft: Sewall letter of Oct. 19, 1884, in Mattison p. 61.
- Stables, cow shed, chicken house, blacksmith shop; Dow as blacksmith: Mattison pp. 62–64; HSR pp. 67–70.
- 1,500 head, May 1885: *Bad Lands Cow Boy*, May 7, 1885, in Mattison p. 54. Contract of June 20, 1885 (1,100 head, $25,000, one-third of increase): SW p. 40.
- Stockmen's Association, Dec. 19, 1884, T.R. chairman: Mattison (1950). Round-up captain 1886: Brooks and Mattison p. 38.
- Boat thieves: RL pp. 114–28; T.R.'s diary in HAG pp. 371–82.
- 60 percent fewer cattle taxed in 1887: Mattison p. 56; Brooks and Mattison p. 41. Sewall's own estimate is "fifty per cent … more" (SW p. 96).
- House open until 1890; sold to Sylvane Ferris 1898; lumber and logs gone: Mattison pp. 57–58.
- NPS research 1948–56; Dee C. Taylor excavation 1959 (cellar, blacksmith shop, oyster cans, sarsaparilla bottles, rubber boots): Harmon pp. 202–203; HSR.
- Park and Elkhorn Ranch Unit created April 25, 1947.
- Darkroom in the cellar: Mattison pp. 61–62, a single recollection by Sewall's daughter; attributed as such.

## Photographs

All historic images are from books published in the U.S. before 1929 (public domain) or the Library of Congress ("no known restrictions"). Modern images are NPS (public domain) or the Library's own July 2025 panoramas.

- *T.R. as photographer:* Hagedorn credits T.R. for "Elkhorn Ranch-House," "The Piazza at Elkhorn," "Corrals at Elkhorn" and "Elkhorn ranch buildings from the river." Sewall prints several of the same negatives without a credit, so those captions say "attributed to." Sewall credits T.R. for the dugout photograph.
- *Buckskin portrait:* LOC dates it 1885; Sewall prints the same pose as 1883. Captioned "about 1885."
- *Then and now (stop 8):* T.R.'s photograph from across the river compared with the view west-northwest (about 252°–318° true) from Library panorama 5. The skyline match is plausible, not certain; the copy says the butte "appears to be" the same.
- *Interlocked antlers:* no public-domain picture exists. Do not caption any image as showing them.

## Dropped

- Cottonwoods "from T.R.'s time": no tree dating exists for the Elkhorn unit.
- "I never would have been President if it had not been for my experiences in North Dakota": not in any first edition. Do not use.
- "The romance of my life began here": only in Hagedorn, unsourced.
- "Cradle of conservation" as an NPS phrase: it is the Forest Service's; use only with attribution.
- Fireplace builder, hearthstone, poorwill: conflicting or unconfirmed.
